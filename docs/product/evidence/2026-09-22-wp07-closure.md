## State and receipt contract

The shared shell now keeps the durable meaning of a governed change visible:

| Durable condition | User-facing presentation | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waiting for a decision | Awaiting approval | `StagedChatApprovals.status.test.tsx` |
| Authorized, not executed | Approved — not yet applied | `StagedChangeCard.blessed.test.tsx` |
| Execution refused | Failed, with the persisted refusal reason and retry availability | `test_staging_failure_reason.py`; `StagedChangeCard.blessed.test.tsx` |
| Applied | Done, with semantic changed-record navigation and Undo where the receipt supports it | `useStagedChange.test.tsx`; live Car Rental journey |
| Rejected or expired | Rejected or Expired | staged-card and approval presentation suites |

The terminal success copy is driven by the consumed receipt rather than the
approval decision. A reload reconciles the token from the durable state and
therefore cannot falsely report a refused execution as a saved change.

## Browser acceptance

The recorded Car Rental Management browser journey proves the ordinary
application shell rather than a synthetic preview:

- one approved resident build produced an App with six expected Tracks;
- Cars rendered the requested fields and seeded row, and a `Status` update
  remained `Rented` after a direct-entry deep-link reload;
- a second principal received the intended commenter surface, scope switching,
  grouped Kanban count, and revoked-access result;
- Service Records rendered its date fields as September 22, 2026 and placed
  the record on that calendar day;
- the App dashboard empty state created Fleet Overview and showed it after
  reload; and
- a final live session read Service Records with its one persisted row and the
  same field values, while the resident transcript showed the terminal
  receipt-backed build completion.

resource identifiers, actions, and observed values.
